C4C-Capture

C4C-Capture (Carbon for Carbon Capture) is a research project funded by the Environmental Protection Agency of Ireland (EPA). It turns Irish biowaste, such as wood residues from forestry and food waste, into porous carbon materials that capture CO₂ directly from the air.

From Irish Biowaste to Carbon Materials

Forestry is Ireland’s largest biomass resource: forests now cover more than 11% of the country, and sawdust and off-cuts from the timber industry are available in large quantities. Food waste and agricultural residues such as straw add further sources. Instead of being burned or sent to landfill, these wastes are converted into graphitic carbon by pyrolysis and hydrothermal carbonisation, using cheap and recyclable iron catalysts.

This also offers a sustainable route to graphite, which the EU lists as a critical raw material. Synthetic graphite is normally made from fossil carbon at temperatures above 2,500 °C, with a large energy and carbon cost.

Better Materials for Capturing CO₂

The graphitic carbons are separated into thin, porous sheets and decorated with amine groups that bind CO₂ selectively, even at the very low concentration found in air (about 420 ppm). Carbon conducts heat far better than the polymer resins used in today’s direct air capture units, so the material can be heated and cooled more quickly to release the captured CO₂. The aim is to cut the energy needed for capture by more than 25%.

The new materials are tested in the group’s laboratory direct air capture prototypes. The capture technology itself is described on the Air-in-Motion and Synaergy pages.

A Circular Bioeconomy for Ireland

Life-cycle assessments run alongside the laboratory work from the start, guiding the choice of waste feedstock, solvents and processes so that the materials themselves have a low carbon footprint. Together with a survey of Ireland’s biowaste resources, they will feed into a policy guidance report for the EPA on how biowaste can be used within an Irish circular carbon economy, including as a source of graphite for other emerging technologies such as batteries.

In this way, C4C-Capture supports Ireland’s Climate Action Plan, which aims to halve greenhouse gas emissions by 2030 and reach net zero by 2050.
