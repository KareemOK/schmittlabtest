C4C-Capture

C4C-Capture (Carbon for Carbon Capture) is a research project funded by the Environmental Protection Agency of Ireland (EPA). It turns Irish biowaste into activated carbons that capture CO₂ directly from the air, giving waste a new, valuable use.

Our Approach

Biowaste is converted into porous activated carbon and functionalised with amines, nitrogen-containing groups that bind CO₂ selectively, even at the very low concentration found in air. Our research focuses on two key targets:

  • CO₂ sorption capacity: how much CO₂ each gram of material can hold.
  • Nitrogen content: loading the carbon with as many amine groups as possible, giving more sites for CO₂ to bind to.

Carbon materials also conduct heat much better than the polymer sorbents used in today’s direct air capture systems. They can therefore be heated and cooled more quickly, reducing the energy needed to release the captured CO₂. The group’s direct air capture technology is described on the Air-in-Motion and Synaergy pages.

A Circular Bioeconomy for Ireland

Life-cycle assessments guide the work so that the materials themselves have a low carbon footprint. Together with a survey of Ireland’s biowaste resources, they will inform a policy report for the EPA on using biowaste within an Irish circular carbon economy.
